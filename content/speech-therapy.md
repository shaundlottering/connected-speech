Speech Therapy
You have something to say. Let's help you say it.

Speech therapy is the process of building the skills someone needs to communicate clearly and confidently, whether that means finding the right sounds, the right words, the right sentence structure, or the right tool to express a thought.

Communication is not just about speech. It is about being understood by the people around you, being able to ask for what you need, and being able to participate in the moments that matter. A conversation at the dinner table, a question in the classroom, a request at work, or a word spoken to someone you love for the very first time.

At Connected Speech, therapy is built around the individual. Not a checklist, not a programme that works the same way for every child or adult who walks through the door. We take the time to understand what is happening, what is driving it, and what a realistic, meaningful path forward looks like for that specific person, in their specific world.

We work with children from birth through school age, and with adults at any stage of life, including those recovering from a stroke, living with a lifelong communication difference, or simply navigating a challenge that has never been properly addressed.

If you have ever wondered whether what you are noticing is something to act on, it probably is. Early intervention consistently leads to better outcomes, and there is no such thing as seeking help too soon.

What speech therapy addresses

Language delays When language milestones are not being met, early intervention makes the biggest difference. We identify what is holding a child back and build the specific skills they need to communicate their needs, connect with others, and thrive at school.

Language and literacy development Strong language skills are the foundation for reading and writing. We build the vocabulary, sentence structure, and phonological awareness a child needs to develop literacy skills and succeed at school.

Articulation and intelligibility Speech that is difficult to understand can affect confidence and connection. We work on accurate sound production and clear speech patterns, so a person can be understood the first time, by anyone.

Stuttering and stammering Stuttering and stammering affect both children and adults, and the impact goes far beyond speech. Your therapist works with you and your family to build fluency, confidence, and the tools to communicate without fear, at any age.

Stroke and medical conditions We support adults recovering from strokes and other medical conditions that affect communication, helping them rebuild the skills they need to express themselves and reconnect with the people in their lives.

Complex communication needs Some people communicate in ways that go beyond speech, and that is okay. We support children and adults with AAC (Augmentative and Alternative Communication), Makaton signing, the PROMPT system, and individualised strategies, so they can express themselves and participate fully in the world around them. With words, with signs, with anything that works.

FAQ

At what age should my child start speech therapy?
There is no minimum age. If you have a concern, a screening is always appropriate, even for very young children. The earlier a communication difference is identified, the more effectively it can be addressed. The 0 to 5 window is a critical period for language development, and early intervention during this time consistently leads to better long-term outcomes. That said, it is never too late. Adults benefit from speech therapy too, at any stage of life.

How do I know if my child needs speech therapy?
Every child develops at their own pace, but there are signs worth paying attention to. By 12 months, most children are pointing, making sounds, and responding to their name. By 18 months, they are typically saying a handful of words. By 2 years, they are starting to combine words. By 3 years, most of what they say should be understandable to unfamiliar people. If your child is not meeting these milestones, or if you simply feel that something is not quite right, a free screening will give you clarity. Trust your instincts. Parents notice things early.

What is the difference between a speech delay and a language delay?
A speech delay refers to difficulty with the physical production of sounds, meaning how words are pronounced. A language delay refers to difficulty with understanding or using language itself, including vocabulary, sentence structure, and the ability to express thoughts and understand others. A child can have one, the other, or both. An assessment will identify exactly what is happening and what kind of support is needed.

My child is two and not talking yet. Should I be worried?
It depends. Some children are late talkers who catch up on their own. Others benefit significantly from early intervention. A free screening will help determine which category your child falls into and whether therapy is recommended. What we know for certain is that waiting and hoping rarely leads to better outcomes than early assessment and, if needed, early support.

Does my child need AAC? Will using AAC stop them from learning to speak?
This is one of the most common concerns parents raise, and the research is clear. AAC does not prevent speech development. In fact, giving a child a reliable way to communicate often reduces frustration and creates the conditions in which speech can emerge. AAC is not a last resort. It is a tool, and for many children it is the bridge between silence and connection.

What is Makaton?
Makaton is a language programme that uses signs and symbols alongside speech to support communication. It is not a replacement for speech. It is used together with spoken words to give people an additional way to express themselves and understand others. Makaton is widely used with children and adults who have communication differences, and it can be learned by the whole family so that communication is consistent across every environment.

What is AAC?
AAC stands for Augmentative and Alternative Communication. It refers to any tool, strategy, or system that supports or replaces speech, from simple picture cards and Makaton signing to high-tech speech-generating devices. AAC is used with children and adults who have complex communication needs, and it is always tailored to the individual. The goal is always functional communication: being able to express needs, wants, thoughts, and feelings in a way that works for that person.

How long will my child need therapy?
This depends entirely on the individual, including the nature of the challenge, the age at which therapy begins, how consistently goals are worked on at home and school, and how the child responds to intervention. Some children make rapid progress over a few months. Others benefit from longer-term support. Your therapist will give you a realistic picture after the assessment and will review progress regularly so that therapy remains purposeful and goal-driven.

What happens in a session?
Sessions are play-based for younger children, because play is how children learn and the best therapy does not feel like work. For older children and adults, sessions are structured around specific, agreed goals using activities and approaches that are relevant to that person's life. After every session, parents and families receive a clear summary of what was worked on and how progress is going.

Can I sit in on my child's sessions?
Yes, and in many cases it is encouraged. Parents who understand what is being worked on and why are far better equipped to support their child between sessions. Carry-over between the therapy room and real life is one of the most important factors in progress, and that happens when families are involved.

Do you work with schools?
Yes. Connected Speech has established school partnerships across the Johannesburg and Sandton area, providing therapy directly on school premises so that children receive support in the environment where they spend most of their day. If your child's school does not currently have a speech therapist on site, get in touch as we may be able to help.

Does medical aid cover speech therapy?
Most major medical aids in South Africa cover speech therapy, including Discovery, Momentum, Bonitas, and Medihelp, typically under chronic benefit, PMB (Prescribed Minimum Benefits), or savings. The specifics depend on your plan. We bill medical aid directly on your behalf, so you do not need to manage the claims process yourself. If you are unsure what your plan covers, contact your scheme and ask specifically about speech therapy benefits.

I am an adult. Is speech therapy relevant for me?
Absolutely. Speech therapy supports adults with a wide range of communication challenges, including stuttering, voice difficulties, communication changes following a stroke or other medical condition, and complex communication needs. Communication affects every area of life, and adults deserve the same access to support as children.

What is the difference between a screening and an assessment?
A screening is a brief, free overview that gives an indication of whether there is a concern worth investigating further. It does not produce a detailed report or diagnosis. An assessment is a comprehensive evaluation that examines specific areas of communication in depth, identifies what is happening and why, and forms the basis of a therapy plan. If a screening suggests that further investigation is warranted, an assessment will be recommended.
